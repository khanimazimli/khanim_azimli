A corporate deck does not need to look corporate.

For this redesign exercise, I took a dense, 49-slide SOX webcast and rebuilt its core story as a focused 10-slide PowerPoint presentation.

The challenge was not decoration. It was deciding what the audience should understand first, what could disappear, and what needed to become visual.

The entire redesign was created around native PowerPoint systems: layouts, editable diagrams, Morph transitions, animation and reusable components.

The result is still a PowerPoint.
It just works much harder.

The information was already there. The redesign focuses on hierarchy, story, clarity, pacing and visual communication.

Thinking, not beautification

What stayed, what merged, what went.

Kept as the story13

source slides carry the argument: methodology, risk assessment, process flow, the litmus test, the identity example, the black box, the changing stack and the conclusion.

Merged3

pairs became single moments: challenge + solution into one Morph, two technology slides into one, process documentation + integrated controls into one data journey.

Left out, on purpose36

agenda, speakers, dividers, legal pages and the ICFR hot topics (code review, IPE failures, agile, user access reviews). Strong content, but a different story.

Master

One slide master, five intentional layouts: Cover, Statement left, Statement full width, Thesis and Closing. Kicker, headline and footer live in the layouts, not on each slide.

Grid

Saved guides at 0.65 in margins, centre lines and a footer baseline. Stations, risk points and controls are distributed on fixed steps.

Morph

Objects share names across slides, like !!System B and !!B Origin, so Morph tracks each one. The progress bar is one Morph object that grows across the whole deck.

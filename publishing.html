Cambridge×Azerbaijan

Adapting an international learning system for a national classroom.

Game Changer was developed as an international English-learning series and adapted into an Azerbaijan edition.

The challenge was not simply translation. The Azerbaijani edition needed to preserve the structure, visual logic and learning rhythm of the original material while working naturally within the requirements of the local educational context.

A textbook page has to manage multiple learning layers at once.

Hierarchy has to remain clear even when vocabulary, grammar, reading, activities, media references and critical-thinking tasks coexist on the same spread.

The design challenge was to make complexity feel navigable.

Game Changer · LocalisationWhat changed for the Azerbaijan edition

Localisation
is also design.

The visual system had to survive localisation.

Every local element had to sit inside the original grid, label system and page rhythm: national front matter, local names and places inside exercises, grammar reference examples and an Azerbaijani imprint.

None of it could look added on. A student should not be able to see where Cambridge ends and the national edition begins.

Designing for
young readers.

Notice

The page must attract attention.

  • 01Section tab in a solid colour field, readable from across the desk.
  • 02A large illustrated scene opens the section before any text.
  • 03Opening questions sit inside the picture, in the students’ own voice.

Understand

Information must be easy to locate and follow.

  • 04“What will I learn?” lists the objectives under the opener.
  • 05Numbered lesson heading, same position on every lesson page.
  • 06Photo row with a caption under each image.
  • 07Subheads phrased as questions break the text into steps.

Remember

Visual structure should support learning.

  • 08“Think, discuss, share”: the same red block in every lesson.
  • 09Running head and folio frame each page with corner brackets.
  • ·Each section ends with a summary diagram (Fig. 2.6).

03

For five consecutive years, I designed the annual yearbook for The International School of Azerbaijan.

Every edition brought together an enormous mix of material: portraits, events, achievements, student life and graduation, in one publication that needed to feel both structured and personal.

This was not a one-off book design. It was an editorial system that had to evolve every year without losing continuity.

The challenge changed every year. The responsibility did not.
